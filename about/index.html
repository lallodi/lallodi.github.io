More verbosely

I am an Associate Professor and head of the Threat Analysis group in the Security Cluster of the Eindhoven University of Technology, in the Netherlands. Previously I worked and studied at the University of Trento, Italy, where I obtained my PhD in 2015 with a thesis entitled: “Risk-based Vulnerability Management. Exploiting the economic nature of the attacker to build sound and measurable vulnerability mitigation strategies”.

My research links the technical, economic, and strategic factors that drive cyber-attackers to defensive operations and technology. Earlier work looked at the optimization problems the attacker solves when engineering a new attack, the economics of vulnerability exploitation, how these translate into new threats at scale, and how attacks are delivered to the (human) user. Today the group works on two lines: cyber threat intelligence, which studies the cybercriminal ecosystem (underground forums, closed markets, Telegram communities) and social engineering, and security operations, which studies analyst processes, analyst training, and the engineering of detection rules in Security Operations Centers. My research draws from computer security, economics, risk analysis, and criminology.

I am a Department Editor of IEEE Security & Privacy magazine. I am an authoring member of the FIRST Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) v3 and v3.1 standard, and the Scientific Director of the Eindhoven Security Hub SOC (ESH-SOC), which is managed by the Threat Analysis group.
